# Who is the first user, and how is Stamina positioned?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 03

## Question

Who is the ideal customer profile for the first 100 users (sport, level, age band, geography, equipment, schedule, willingness to pay), what problem does Stamina solve for them that they currently solve badly, and what is the one-line positioning against Fitbod, MyFitnessPal and Future (see the competitor teardown ticket)? Output: ICP statement, problem statement, positioning statement, and the anti-persona (who Stamina is explicitly not for).

## Comments

  - ICP segment: recreational competitors, adults playing a weekly sport with games/matches (basketball, 5-a-side, padel, volleyball, tennis) who want training and eating fitted around them. Not general gym-goers, weight-loss-only, or endurance.

  - Age: 18+ hard floor; focus 25–45.

  - Problem: no plan reacts to their game schedule; generic apps don't know a game happened; they stitch Fitbod, MFP, YouTube and guesswork.

## Answer

**ICP (first 100 users)**: adult amateur athletes, 18+ (focus 25–45), intermediate (≥ 1 year active), in Greece (Athens first, EN/EL; EU-open, no US marketing until post-MVP), who play a sport regularly (racket: padel, tennis; team: basketball, 5-a-side football, beach volleyball; wind/water: kitesurfing, windsurfing; etc.), planned or weather-dependent, and want training and eating fitted around it. Gym or basic home kit; 2–4 Sessions × 30–60 min/week on top of their sport. Team players with a coach are in: practices and games are Activities, and Stamina fills strength, nutrition and recovery gaps ("training, nutrition, or both"). Willingness-to-pay hypothesis to test: €5–10/month.

**Problem**: amateur athletes fit gym work and eating around their sport with no plan that reacts to what they actually played. Generic apps don't know a game or a windy afternoon happened, so they train hard before playing, under-recover after, and eat the same on sport days as rest days. Today they stitch together Fitbod, MyFitnessPal, YouTube and guesswork.

**Positioning**: *For amateur athletes, Stamina is the AI coach that plans your training, food and recovery around the sport you play, and adapts when you've played.* Unlike Fitbod (gym-only, blind to your sport, no nutrition), MyFitnessPal (logs food, no plan) and Future ($199/month, US-only, no meal plans).

- **Lead differentiator**: sport-aware Adaptation. An Activity (logged, or imported from a health app) produces a suggested Program change. Four-Pillar coverage and the daily Check-in support it.
- **Category**: "AI training & nutrition coach for amateur athletes".
- **Banned claim words** (app, store page, ads): prevent injury, treat, heal, rehab, diagnose, therapy, hormone, testosterone, medical. Use performance and recovery language instead.

**First users from**: ~30 from Vang's own network (padel, beach volleyball, kite, CrossFit, team-sport contacts). The rest come from clubs, schools and leagues across the ICP sports (planned in Success metrics and go-to-market for the first 100 users).

**Anti-persona**:
- **Screened out and referred to a professional** (exact rules in Safety rules): under-18, pregnant, injury rehab, known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease.
- **Not targeted**: physique-focused bodybuilders, strict calorie trackers, elite athletes with support staff, general gym-goers with no sport, weight-loss-only users, endurance specialists.

Propagated: MVP scope (sport list core), Monetization and pricing (WTP hypothesis), Success metrics and go-to-market (channels), Training generation model (unplanned Activities). `GLOSSARY.md`: Session is what Stamina schedules; Activity covers planned and unplanned sport (a "kite session" is an Activity).
