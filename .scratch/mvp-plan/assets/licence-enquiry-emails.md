# Licence enquiry emails

For: Ask PAR-Q+ Collaboration and CSEP for in-app licence terms (`../issues/29-screening-licence-terms.md`).

Fill the two bracketed fields (`[Full name]`, `[Postal address]`) and send from your own address. Each email covers the fields its licensor's permissions page asks for:

- PAR-Q+ terms page: purpose, instruments and versions, proposed modifications, method of administration, anticipated users, commercial nature. Source: https://eparmedx.com/about/terms-and-conditions/
- CSEP permissions page: name and address, title of the publication, estimated publication date and cost, the CSEP work, and an example of how it will appear. Source: https://csep.ca/contact-us/permissions/

---

## 1. PAR-Q+ Collaboration

**To:** eparmedx@gmail.com
**Subject:** Permission request: PAR-Q+ 2025 in a commercial fitness coaching app (EN, Greece/EU)

Dear PAR-Q+ Collaboration,

I am an independent developer in Athens, Greece, building a mobile fitness and nutrition coaching app for adult amateur athletes (working name "Stamina"). Before anyone starts a training program, I would like to screen them with the PAR-Q+, and I am writing to request authorization under your Terms and Conditions.

- **Purpose**: pre-participation screening at sign-up, before the app suggests any exercise. The app is a general fitness and wellness product. It does not diagnose, treat or rehabilitate, and it is not a medical device.
- **Instrument and version**: PAR-Q+ 2025 (form dated 01-11-2024), page 1 plus the follow-up questions. No ePARmed-X+ integration. Users who need it would be referred to your official online version.
- **Proposed modifications**: none to wording, order or pathways. The form would be presented screen by screen in the app, with your copyright notice, version and acknowledgement shown.
- **Method of administration**: self-completed electronically by the user on a phone. Answers are stored with the user's explicit consent under GDPR. A "YES" pathway either stops exercise suggestions or limits them, and directs the user to a health care practitioner or qualified exercise professional as the form instructs. We would produce no risk score.
- **Anticipated users**: adults aged 18 and over only. About 100 in a free invite-only beta in Greece from early 2027, then a gradual EU rollout, possibly several thousand users in the first year.
- **Commercial nature**: commercial. Free during the beta, then a consumer subscription (around €5–10/month).
- **Languages**: English at launch. The app's users are Greek, and I understand a Greek translation is underway.

My questions:

1. Would you authorize this use, and what licence term, conditions and fees would apply?
2. Is there an expected date for the official Greek translation? Until then, may Greek-speaking users complete the English version in the app?
3. If we instead **link out** to your official online PAR-Q+ and the user then confirms their result in the app, does that also require authorization?
4. What turnaround should I expect for a decision?

Thank you for maintaining these instruments. I'm happy to provide screenshots or a demo of the intended flow.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
[Postal address]

## 2. CSEP Permissions

**To:** info@csep.ca
**Subject:** CSEP Permissions: licence enquiry for the Get Active Questionnaire in a mobile app

Dear CSEP Permissions,

I am requesting information on a CSEP Licensing Agreement to reproduce the **Get Active Questionnaire (GAQ, 2017)** in software.

- **Name and address**: [Full name], [Postal address]. I am an independent developer and publisher.
- **Title of the publication**: "Stamina" (working name), a mobile fitness and nutrition coaching app for adult amateur athletes. It is a general wellness product and not a medical device.
- **Estimated publication date and cost**: a free invite-only beta in Greece from early 2027, then a consumer subscription of about €5–10/month across the EU.
- **CSEP work**: the Get Active Questionnaire, unmodified and in its entirety, with the credit "The Canadian Society for Exercise Physiology, www.csep.ca/guidelines". We would also link to the GAQ Reference Document. Pregnant users are referred out of the app, so we do not plan to use the GAQ for Pregnancy.
- **How it will appear**: each GAQ question is shown on screen, unmodified, completed by the user on a phone at sign-up. Answers are stored with the user's explicit consent under GDPR. A "YES" pathway either stops exercise suggestions or limits them, and directs the user to a health care or qualified exercise professional as the GAQ instructs. Users are adults aged 18 and over, about 100 in the beta and possibly several thousand in the first year.

My questions:

1. What are the terms and fees of a CSEP Licensing Agreement for this software use? Does the $200-per-item permission fee apply in addition?
2. Is there an official Greek version of the GAQ, or would a translation need CSEP's approval? I'm aware the GAQ for Pregnancy exists in Greek.
3. What licence term would apply, and what turnaround should I expect for a decision?

Thank you. I'm happy to provide screenshots or a demo of the intended flow.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
[Postal address]
