# Screening and red-flag sources: PAR-Q+ licensing, ACSM screening

Ticket: `.scratch/mvp-plan/issues/10-screening-sources.md`. All sources accessed 2026-10-28. Pages on `csep.ca` timed out from this network on that date, so CSEP pages are cited from Wayback Machine snapshots (the snapshot date is given with each one). Anything marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be checked against a primary source. Statements marked **[INFERENCE]** are my own reasoning and are not quoted from any source.

## Answer

- **The PAR-Q+ (current edition "2025", form dated 01-11-2024) cannot go into a commercial app without permission.** The PAR-Q+ Collaboration's terms require *prior written authorization* before the PAR-Q+ or ePARmed-X+ is incorporated into "mobile applications, websites, commercial software, automated screening systems". The terms also reserve the right to charge fees for commercial and electronic use. Free use covers only unmodified forms for "individual, clinical, educational, and non-commercial research purposes". The official online version is a UBC-hosted Qualtrics survey, which Stamina could link to. No Greek version exists yet; the Collaboration lists Greek as "underway". [S1–S4]
- **The original PAR-Q (CSEP, revised 2002) is retired.** It covered ages 15–69 only and said "No changes permitted… photocopy… only if you use the entire form". CSEP says its Get Active Questionnaire (GAQ, 2017) "replaces previous tools". [S5, S7]
- **The CSEP GAQ, its Pregnancy version (GAQ-P) and its Reference Document are free to reproduce if unmodified and complete.** Selling them or putting them in software needs CSEP's written permission and a licence fee. Reproducing them "in computer software" requires a CSEP Licensing Agreement. The GAQ-P already exists in Greek. [S6–S9]
- **The ACSM pre-participation algorithm (Riebe et al. 2015) is copyrighted text and figures.** The journal is paywalled, and reuse goes through Wolters Kluwer / Copyright Clearance Center. The algorithm's logic is public: (1) is the person currently exercising, (2) do they have known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, or signs and symptoms of it, (3) what intensity do they want. The ACSM 12th edition (2025) changelog lists no change to the screening chapter beyond a new title and an incident-report figure. [S10–S14]
- **The Australian APSS (ESSA, AUSactive and SMA) is a free 6-question Stage-1 form for adults**, with a separate version for ages 5–17 (PSS-YP). The form does not state any licence terms (**UNVERIFIED**). The companion eBook is "All rights reserved". [S15–S18]
- **The red-flag symptoms are the same across sources:** chest, neck, jaw or arm pain or discomfort; fainting, dizziness or loss of consciousness; breathlessness at rest or with mild effort; palpitations; ankle swelling; leg pain when walking (claudication); unusual fatigue; poorly controlled diabetes or low blood sugar; blood pressure ≥160/90; recent concussion. Pregnancy adds vaginal bleeding, leaking fluid, regular painful contractions, calf pain or swelling, headache and muscle weakness. [S1, S7, S9, S11, S15, S19–S21]
- **[INFERENCE] Trade-off:** shipping PAR-Q+ or GAQ text inside the app needs a written licence from a third party, which may cost money and has no guaranteed turnaround. Copyright covers how a text is worded, not the ideas behind it (WIPO Copyright Treaty art. 2 [S22]). So writing Stamina's own questions from the published criteria avoids the licence, but it gives up the "validated instrument" claim. Both licensors forbid presenting modified versions as official. Get legal advice before relying on this.

## 2. How each instrument decides who is cleared

| Instrument | Questions | Decision logic | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PAR-Q+ page 1 | 7 yes/no: (1) heart condition or high blood pressure diagnosed by a doctor; (2) chest pain at rest, during daily activities or during physical activity; (3) loss of balance from dizziness, or loss of consciousness in the last 12 months ("answer NO if your dizziness was associated with over-breathing"); (4) another chronic condition; (5) prescribed medication for a chronic condition; (6) bone, joint or soft-tissue problem in the last 12 months that activity could worsen; (7) doctor said activity should be medically supervised only | All NO: "cleared for physical activity", but "If you are over the age of 45 yr and NOT accustomed to regular vigorous to maximal effort exercise, consult a qualified exercise professional" before that intensity. Any YES: complete pages 2–3 (10 condition groups). Any YES on a follow-up question: ePARmed-X+ and/or a Qualified Exercise Professional, which gives low, intermediate or high risk. Clearance lasts at most 12 months and "becomes invalid if my condition changes" | S1, S2 |
| ACSM 2015 algorithm | (a) Currently exercising: "planned, structured physical activity at least 30 min at moderate intensity on at least 3 d∙wk−1 for at least the last 3 months". (b) Known CV disease (cardiac, peripheral vascular, cerebrovascular), metabolic disease (type 1 and 2 diabetes) or renal disease. (c) Signs or symptoms (see §4). (d) Desired intensity | **Not exercising:** no disease and no symptoms → light to moderate, progress gradually. Known disease without symptoms → medical clearance before any intensity. Symptoms → clearance. **Exercising:** no disease and no symptoms → continue moderate or vigorous. Known disease without symptoms → continue moderate, clearance before vigorous. Symptoms → stop and get clearance. Cardiovascular risk-factor profiling "is no longer included" | S10, S11, S12 |
| CSEP GAQ | 4 questions (Q1 has parts A–F, covering the last 6 months): heart disease or stroke, or chest pain/pressure; high blood pressure or resting BP ≥160/90; dizziness during activity; shortness of breath at rest; loss of consciousness; concussion. Q2 pain or swelling; Q3 provider advised avoiding or modifying activity; Q4 other condition. "If you are unsure about any question, answer YES." | All NO → assess current activity. If below guidelines and wanting vigorous activity, consult a QEP. Any YES → Reference Document gives advice per item. User declares either that they consulted someone or that they are "comfortable with becoming more physically active on my own" | S7, S8 |
| APSS Stage 1 | 6 questions: heart condition or stroke; unexplained chest pain or discomfort; faint, dizzy or losing balance during activity; asthma attack needing medical attention in the last 12 months; trouble controlling blood sugar in the last 3 months (diabetics); other conditions (examples: acute injury, pregnancy, epilepsy, transplant, cancer) | Any YES → seek guidance from an allied health professional or medical practitioner. All NO → weighted weekly minutes: under 150 means light to moderate only; 150 or more means continue current intensity. Stage 2 (risk factors) is completed with a professional | S15, S16 |

## 4. Red-flag symptoms, with sources

Two kinds of list exist. **Pre-participation flags** mean "see a professional before starting or increasing exercise". **Stop flags** mean "stop now and get medical attention". Both are listed below.

### 4a. General population (cardiovascular, metabolic and other)

| Red flag | Stated as | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Pain or discomfort in the chest, neck, jaw, arms or other areas "that may result from ischemia", at rest or during activity | ACSM sign/symptom → medical clearance | S11 (ACSM 2015 figure footnote, reproduced with permission) |
| New chest discomfort, or a change in its pattern at rest or during exercise | ACSM-EP competency: "may require consultation with medical personnel" | S24 |
| Chest pain at rest, during daily activities, or during physical activity | PAR-Q+ Q2 → follow-up | S1 |
| Chest pain or pressure that is new | GAQ Reference Document: "talk to a doctor" | S8 |
| Unexplained chest pain or discomfort at rest or during exercise | APSS Q2 → seek guidance before exercising | S15, S16 |
| Dizziness or fainting (syncope) | ACSM sign/symptom | S11 |
| Loss of balance from dizziness, or loss of consciousness in the last 12 months (dizziness from over-breathing excluded) | PAR-Q+ Q3 | S1 |
| Dizziness or light-headedness during physical activity | GAQ Q1C: consult a provider and "refrain from increasing the intensity" | S7, S8 |
| Loss of consciousness or fainting "for any reason" | GAQ Q1E: "consult a doctor" before becoming more active | S7, S8 |
| Feeling faint, dizzy or losing balance during exercise | APSS Q3 | S15 |
| Shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion; unusual fatigue or breathlessness with usual activities | ACSM sign/symptom | S11 |
| Shortness of breath at rest | GAQ Q1D: see a doctor if not relieved by medication | S7, S8 |
| Extreme breathlessness at rest or during exercise | ACSM-EP competency (lung-related) | S24 |
| Breathlessness when lying flat (orthopnea) or waking breathless at night (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea) | ACSM sign/symptom | S11 |
| Ankle swelling (oedema) | ACSM sign/symptom | S11, S23 |
| Palpitations or a racing heart (tachycardia) | ACSM sign/symptom | S11, S23 |
| Leg pain when walking (intermittent claudication) | ACSM sign/symptom | S11, S23, S24 |
| Known heart murmur | ACSM sign/symptom | S11, S23 |
| Resting BP ≥160/90 mmHg, or unknown BP in a person with hypertension | PAR-Q+ 4b; GAQ Q1B (medical clearance) | S1, S7, S8 |
| Hypoglycaemia after exercise or during daily life (shakiness, sweating, confusion, etc.); poorly controlled blood glucose | PAR-Q+ 5a–5b; APSS Q5 | S1, S15 |
| Concussion, or loss of consciousness from a head injury, in the last 12 months | PAR-Q+ 10a; GAQ Q1F (provider decides when to resume) | S1, S7, S8 |
| Asthma symptoms (chest tightness, wheeze, cough more than 2 days a week, or rescue inhaler more than twice a week); asthma attack needing medical attention in the last 12 months | PAR-Q+ 7c; APSS Q4 | S1, S15 |
| New pain or swelling that limits activity | GAQ Q2: "If this swelling or pain is new, consult a health care provider" | S8 |
| Temporary illness such as a cold or fever | PAR-Q+, PAR-Q, GAQ: delay becoming more active | S1, S5, S7 |
| Health changes after clearance | PAR-Q+ (clearance becomes invalid); GAQ (complete it again) | S1, S7 |

Background: the ACSM abstract says "exercise-related cardiovascular events are often preceded by warning signs/symptoms" [S10]. The AHA 2007 statement lists "promptly evaluating possible prodromal symptoms" as a prudent strategy, though one that has "not been systematically evaluated" [S25]. ACSM/AHA criteria for stopping an exercise *test* (GETP chapter 4; AHA 2013 *Exercise Standards*) are paywalled and were not read (**UNVERIFIED**).

## 5. Not verified

- Full text of Riebe et al. 2015 and of GETP 10th–12th edition chapter 2 (both paywalled). The algorithm details above come from the figure and footnotes as reproduced "with permission" in Whitfield et al. 2017 (a PMC author manuscript with Riebe-paper co-author Whitfield) [S11], plus the 12th-edition crosswalk [S13]. Whether ACSM addresses pregnancy or under-18s in its *screening* chapter is unverified.
- What the ePARmed-X+ asks about pregnancy (it is an interactive Qualtrics survey).
- The licence status of the APSS and PSS-YP forms, which state none. The legacy PAR-Q's copyright line (the converted PDF showed only the liability clause naming CSEP and Health Canada).
- 2020 ESC Guidelines on sports cardiology (Pelliccia et al., *Eur Heart J* 2021;42:17-96) [S27]. This is the main European cardiology source on pre-participation evaluation and exercise with CVD, but the full text was blocked (Cloudflare/403), so none of its content is used here.
- Whether *linking out* to the official UBC-hosted PAR-Q+ online form counts as "incorporation" under the PAR-Q+ terms. The terms don't address linking ([INFERENCE]: probably not incorporation; ask eparmedx@gmail.com to confirm).
