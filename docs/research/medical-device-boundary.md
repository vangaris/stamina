# Medical-device boundary for adaptive coaching software

Ticket: `.scratch/mvp-plan/issues/04-medical-device-boundary.md`. Research date / access date for every source: **2026-10-28**.
Scope: EU MDR 2017/745 (Art. 2, Annex VIII Rule 11) + MDCG 2019-11 rev.1, US FD&C Act §520(o) (21st Century Cures Act §3060) + FDA General Wellness and device-software guidance, UK MHRA software guidance. Vocabulary follows `GLOSSARY.md` (Program, Pillar, Block, Session, Check-in, Log, Adaptation).

## Answer

- **Intended purpose decides it, not the algorithm or how risky it is.** Under all three regimes, software becomes a medical device when the manufacturer intends it for a medical purpose. That purpose is read from the label, the instructions, the UI, the app-store text and category, ads, social media and repeated testimonials. A disclaimer cannot undo a medical claim made somewhere else ([MDR Art. 2(12)][mdr-oj]; [MHRA p.11][mhra]; [FDA GW 2026 §III][fda-gw]). MDCG states that the risk of harm "is not a criterion" for qualification ([MDCG 2019-11 §3.1][mdcg]).
- **Stays wellness:** generating and adapting a Program for general fitness, strength, endurance, sport performance, body composition or weight management, general mobility, recovery, sleep habits and stress. That includes Adaptation driven by RPE, sleep and soreness Check-ins, plus calorie and macro targets, meal plans and Log trends. All three regulators name these explicitly as non-device fitness or wellness functions ([MDCG §3.1][mdcg]; [FDA MMA App. A #6][fda-mma]; [FDA GW §III][fda-gw]; [MHRA p.12, p.21][mhra]).
- **Crosses the line:** anything aimed at a **specific disease, injury or disability**, whether it diagnoses, monitors, treats, alleviates or (for disease) prevents or predicts it. Examples: rehab or return-from-injury protocols, exercises chosen to relieve pain, telling the user what their pain probably is, symptom matching or likelihood ranking, individual disease-risk scores, diets for a named disease, insulin or medication dosing, and alerts that use clinical thresholds or call a value "abnormal".
- **EU is the strictest regime.** The EU has nothing like FDA's enforcement discretion or its "may help living well with [chronic disease]" wellness category. Any disease-directed function is medical device software, and Rule 11 makes most of it **Class IIa or higher, which needs a Notified Body** ([MDR Annex VIII Rule 11, Art. 52(6)][mdr-oj]). An EU-first product therefore cannot use the US wellness latitude for disease-adjacent coaching.
- **"See a professional" signposting is allowed** if it stays generic: no named condition, no "abnormal" or diagnostic framing, no clinical thresholds, and no ongoing disease monitoring ([FDA GW 2026 §III][fda-gw]; [MHRA symptom-checker appendix][mhra-sym]). PAR-Q-style screening that only gates the Program or refers the user on fits this pattern. Turning the answers into a risk score or a likely condition does not.
- **Grey zones:**
  - **"Prevents injury."** The EU and UK definitions list *prevention* only for disease, and MHRA says injury-prevention claims fall outside the prevention limb. FDA's position was not found.
  - **Programming around a user-reported injury by excluding exercises.** No source covers this directly.
  - **Pregnancy-specific guidance.** FDA lists motivational guidance for pregnant people under enforcement discretion, so it may be a device in the US.

## 1. The legal test in each jurisdiction

| Jurisdiction | Legal hook | Software is a device when… | Explicit wellness carve-out | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU | MDR Art. 2(1) "medical device" includes software intended for "diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease" or "diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of, or compensation for, an injury or disability", or "investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological or pathological process or state" | …the manufacturer intends it for one of those *specific medical purposes*. Intended purpose (Art. 2(12)) comes from "the label, … instructions for use or … promotional or sales materials or statements" | Recital 19: "software intended for life-style and well-being purposes is not a medical device". MDCG 2019-11 §3.1: "wellness or fitness apps, do not qualify as MDSW" | [MDR OJ text][mdr-oj], [MDR consolidated 10.01.2025][mdr-cons], [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 §3.1][mdcg] |
| EU (case law) | CJEU C-219/11 *Brain Products* (interpreting predecessor Directive 93/42/EEC, same limb wording) | The "investigation of a physiological process" limb applies "only if it is intended for a medical purpose". Training that changes physiology is therefore not caught by that wording alone | — | [C-219/11][cjeu-brain] |
| EU (case law) | CJEU C-329/16 *Snitem* (interpreting Directive 93/42/EEC; MHRA guidance still cites it) | Software "of which at least one of the functions makes it possible to use patient-specific data for the purposes, inter alia, of detecting contraindications, drug interactions and excessive doses, is, in respect of that function, a medical device" | — | [C-329/16][cjeu-snitem], [MHRA p.19][mhra] |
| EU | MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 (June 2025) decision steps 3–4 | The software acts on data beyond storage, communication or simple search (step 3), **and** the action is "for the benefit of individual patients". Generic pathways "not directed to individual patients" are excluded (step 4) | Same as above | [MDCG Figure 1 decision steps 3–4][mdcg] |
| US | FD&C Act §201(h) device definition + §520(o)(1)(B), added by Cures Act §3060 | §201(h): intended for "diagnosis of disease or other conditions, or in the cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease", or "to affect the structure or any function of the body" | §520(o)(1)(B) excludes software "for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle and is unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease or condition". The General Wellness guidance (Jan 2026) adds non-enforcement for the second category ("may help reduce the risk of / living well with" a chronic disease) | [Pub. L. 114-255 §3060][cures-law], [FDA Cures §520(o) guidance §IV.B][fda-cures], [FDA GW 2026][fda-gw] |
| US | FDA *Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications* (Sept 2022) | §V.A item 3: software that performs "patient-specific analysis and provide[s] patient-specific diagnosis or treatment recommendations to patients, caregivers, or other users who are not health care professionals" | App. A #6: software to "log, record, track, evaluate, or make decisions or behavioral suggestions related to developing or maintaining general fitness, health or wellness" is **not a device** | [FDA MMA guidance][fda-mma] |
| UK (GB) | UK MDR 2002 reg. 2: "diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease", "diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of or compensation for an injury or handicap" | …it has a medical purpose, judged from labelling, IFU and promotional material, including "App store description and category", the landing page and social media | "The monitoring of general fitness, general health and general wellbeing is not usually considered to be a medical purpose". "Apps and software for monitoring sport or fitness purposes, e.g. heart rate, are not considered to be medical devices" | [UK MDR 2002 reg. 2][uk-mdr], [MHRA software guidance v1.10g pp.11–12, 21][mhra] |

### What crossing the line costs (decision-relevant)

| Regime | Consequence once qualified | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| EU | Rule 11: software "intended to provide information which is used to take decisions with diagnosis or therapeutic purposes" is **Class IIa**, or IIb/III if a wrong decision could cause serious or irreversible harm. MDCG: sub-rule 11a "is generally applicable to all MDSW". Only Rule 11c ("all other software") is Class I. Class IIa needs Annex IX conformity assessment, i.e. a Notified Body (Art. 52(6)). Class I is self-declared (Art. 52(7)). A therapy loop that adapts to diagnostic feedback was classed **Class III under Rule 22** in MDCG's example (cognitive therapy) | [MDR Annex VIII Rule 11, Art. 52][mdr-cons], [MDCG §4.2.1, Annex IV][mdcg] |
| EU | Claims about an intended medical purpose must be backed by clinical evidence, or the software "may not be CE marked as a medical device, nor present said claims" | [MDCG §3][mdcg] |
| UK | UKCA (or accepted CE) marking. All devices must be registered with MHRA. Class IIa and above need an Approved Body. Clinical evaluation is required. For lay users, "provision of an indicative diagnosis may be enough to imply that the device is allowing direct diagnosis" (→ Class IIa) | [MHRA pp.26–28][mhra], [MHRA App. 1][mhra-sym] |
| US | Device controls apply by class: registration and listing, QMSR (21 CFR 820), labeling, MDR adverse-event reporting, and premarket review as classified | [FDA MMA §VI, App. D][fda-mma] |

## 2. Do / don't table: product behaviour

Legend: **Do** = stays wellness in all three regimes. **Don't** = crosses into medical-device territory in at least the EU or UK, and usually all three. **Grey** = no primary source addresses it directly, or the regimes differ. Conservative patterns are listed as options, not decisions.

| Area (Pillar / feature) | Do: stays wellness | Don't: crosses the line | Grey: facts and trade-offs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Program generation (Training)** | Generate and adapt Blocks and Sessions for general fitness, strength, endurance, padel performance and coordination from goals, sport, equipment and schedule | Generating a Program *for* a disease, injury or disability (e.g. "for knee osteoarthritis", "post-ACL", "for hypertension") | — | FDA GW §III: "improve physical fitness, develop or improve endurance, strength or coordination". FDA MMA App. A #6: "make exercise or posture suggestions". [MDCG §3.1][mdcg], [FDA GW][fda-gw], [FDA MMA][fda-mma] |
| **Adaptation from Check-ins (RPE, sleep, soreness)** | Adjust volume or intensity, add deloads, swap Sessions based on RPE, sleep and soreness, framed as training load and recovery. Show the user's own values, trends and baselines | Interpreting Check-ins as a health state (e.g. "signs of overtraining syndrome / illness / depression"). Clinical thresholds. Calling a value "abnormal". Alerts that recommend clinical action | No primary source rules on DOMS-style "soreness" specifically [INFERENCE: soreness as a training-load signal sits inside the "recovery" wellness domain FDA names] | FDA GW 2026: outputs may be "contextualize[d] … in relation to sleep, activity, stress, recovery". Disqualifiers: "references to specific diseases, clinical conditions, or diagnostic thresholds" and "alerts, alarms, or prompts that recommend or require specific clinical action". [FDA GW][fda-gw]. MHRA: fitness monitoring is not a device. [MHRA p.21][mhra] |
| **Injuries reported by the user** | Let the user flag a body area to avoid. Exclude or limit exercises that load it. Tell them to get professional advice before training it | **Rehab protocols**, return-to-play or return-from-surgery plans, exercises selected to heal or restore an injured structure, progressing an injured area based on pain reports | Working around a reported injury by *exclusion only* is not addressed by any source found [INFERENCE: it stays closest to wellness if no therapeutic intent is claimed or implied in UI or copy] | MDCG §3.2: MDSW "intended to alleviate pain associated with the musculoskeletal pathology by recommending personalised rehabilitation exercises" = MDSW. FDA GW: claims "to restore a structure or function impaired due to a disease or condition" are not wellness. FDA MMA App. B #4: video games motivating "physical therapy exercises at home" = enforcement discretion, i.e. MAY be a device. [MDCG][mdcg], [FDA GW][fda-gw], [FDA MMA][fda-mma] |
| **Pain** | Treat reported pain as a stop or modify signal: stop the exercise, swap it, and suggest seeing a professional | **Diagnosing pain**: asking symptom questions to identify a cause, naming a likely condition, ranking possibilities by likelihood, filtering by severity, recommending treatment for it | — | MHRA: "Apps and software that provide medical condition advice based on user entered data" may be devices. Symptom checkers that output matching conditions, likelihood, treatment or "red flag" severity filters may be devices; ones that "only signpost the user to suitable care" are unlikely to be. [MHRA p.20][mhra], [MHRA App. 1][mhra-sym]. FDA MMA App. B #7–#8 (symptom checklists → possible conditions or care setting) = enforcement discretion. [FDA MMA][fda-mma] |
| **Injury prevention** | Generic education (warm-ups, progressive loading, rest) given as tips | An individual injury-risk *score or prediction* computed from the user's physiological data | EU and UK definitions list *prevention* for **disease only**, not injury. MHRA: the prevention limb "does not include products that claim to prevent injury or handicap", and tips or advice on prevention are unlikely to be devices. MDCG puts a device that prevents "illnesses or pathologies by analysing physiological parameters (e.g. placement of the dorsal vertebrae …)" in Class IIa. No FDA text on injury-prevention claims for training software was found (**unverified**) | [MDR Art. 2(1)][mdr-oj], [MHRA p.19][mhra], [MDCG §4.2.1][mdcg] |
| **Onboarding screening (PAR-Q-style questions)** | Present a standard pre-exercise questionnaire. Use answers only to gate or limit the Program and to show a generic "talk to a doctor before starting" message | Turning answers into an **individual disease-risk score** ("X% risk of heart disease"), naming a likely condition, issuing "medical clearance" | EU guidance does not address exercise-readiness questionnaires specifically (**not found**). FDA MMA App. B #6 ("patient-specific screening, counseling, and preventive recommendations from well-known and established authorities") = enforcement discretion, i.e. MAY be a device in the US. *Snitem* makes contraindication detection on patient-specific data a device function for **medicines**. Applying that logic to exercise contraindications is untested [INFERENCE] | MHRA: individual risk "based on entered data for that patient" may be a device; "general recommendations to seek further advice" unlikely. Decision support offering "only lifestyle treatment choices or referral advice (e.g. see your GP)" unlikely. [MHRA pp.12, 20][mhra]. MDCG §6.1: app giving "a 10-year risk of cardiovascular disease from data input by a lay user" = MDSW. [MDCG][mdcg], [C-329/16][cjeu-snitem], [FDA MMA][fda-mma] |
| **Users with medical conditions** (diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, eating disorders, pregnancy) | Use the condition as an exclusion or limiting flag plus generic signposting | Tailoring the Program or nutrition *to manage* the condition, or claiming it helps living with or reducing the risk of the disease (EU/UK) | US only: "may help reduce the risk of / may help living well with" a chronic disease is a wellness category that FDA does not enforce, but it is **not** excluded from the device definition. Coaching patients "with conditions such as cardiovascular disease, hypertension, diabetes, or obesity" = enforcement discretion. Pregnancy: "motivational guidance to pregnant people" = enforcement discretion, i.e. may be a device. MDCG 2019-11 contains no equivalent EU carve-out | [FDA GW §III][fda-gw], [FDA Cures §IV.B.2][fda-cures], [FDA MMA §V.B, App. B #2][fda-mma]. MDCG: eating-disorder app reacting to "diet, physical activity, body image" = MDSW. [MDCG §3.2][mdcg] |
| **Nutrition** | Calorie, macro and hydration targets for weight management, body composition or performance. Meal planners, recipes, food Logs, calorie counters, "healthy eating" suggestions | **Disease-specific nutrition** (diabetic, renal, IBS, "to lower blood pressure"), "treat obesity", eating-disorder treatment, **insulin or medication dosing** from food intake | — | FDA MMA App. A #6: "dietary logs, calorie counters, or make dietary suggestions", "meal planners and recipes". FDA GW Example 3 (diet tracking for weight management) not a device. FDA GW: "treat or diagnose obesity" and "treat an eating disorder" are not wellness claims. MHRA: insulin dose from meal carbohydrate = may be a device. MDCG §3.2: insulin dose recommendations = MDSW. [FDA MMA][fda-mma], [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA p.22][mhra], [MDCG][mdcg] |
| **Mobility & Recovery** | Mobility and flexibility routines, recovery Sessions, deloads, "improve general mobility" | "Relieve back pain", "restore range of motion after injury or surgery", correcting a pathology (e.g. scoliosis, disc problems) | — | FDA GW: "improve general mobility" is wellness. Restoring a structure or function "impaired due to a disease or condition" is not. MHRA indicative words include "Reduce pain", "Heals". [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA p.22][mhra] |
| **Daily Routine (sleep, stress, habits)** | Sleep-hygiene tips, sleep trend Logs, relaxation, stress-management prompts, habit nudges, motivational tips | "Treat insomnia", detecting sleep apnoea, managing anxiety, depression or another diagnosed psychiatric condition | — | FDA GW: "sleep management", "relaxation or stress management" are wellness. "Helps treat an anxiety disorder" is not. FDA MMA App. A #6: "Help healthy people track … normal sleep patterns". App. C: sleep-apnoea diagnosis = device. MDCG: depression-management and depression-scoring software = MDSW (Class IIb for scoring). MHRA: "non-specific stress" is unlikely to be a device. [FDA GW][fda-gw], [FDA MMA][fda-mma], [MDCG Annex IV][mdcg], [MHRA p.22][mhra] |
| **Escalation / red-flag messages** | Generic "stop and seek medical help" or "a doctor or physio may be able to help" notices that name no condition, set no threshold and do not monitor over time | Messages that identify or name a disease, call an output abnormal or pathological, include clinical thresholds or treatment advice, or run ongoing monitoring of a condition | EU texts contain no explicit provision on signposting (**not found**). UK and US texts converge on "signposting only is fine" | FDA GW 2026: a notification that "evaluation by a healthcare professional may be helpful" is compatible with wellness if it does not "identify or name a specific disease", does not characterise output "as abnormal, pathological, or diagnostic", contains no "clinical thresholds, diagnoses, or treatment recommendations", and has no "ongoing alerts or monitoring intended to manage a disease". [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA App. 1][mhra-sym] |
| **Data display & sharing** | Show the user's own Logs, trends and summaries. Export or share with a coach or physio | Analysing that data to support someone else's clinical decision (e.g. reports that flag clinical risk for a clinician) | — | MHRA: an app that "simply replace[s] a written diary/log of symptoms" is unlikely to be a device, though "features that enhance the data presented may bring it into the remit". FDA MMA App. A #9: record data to share with a professional = not a device. [MHRA p.21][mhra], [FDA MMA][fda-mma] |
| **AI / chat coach (if used)** | Constrained to training, nutrition and habit coaching within the wellness scope above | Free-text answers that diagnose, give treatment, interpret symptoms or dose medication or supplements for a user | [INFERENCE] Generated text is part of the UI and functionality that regulators read when judging intended purpose, so scope limits must hold in output, not only in marketing | FDA GW 2026 judges "labeling, advertising, user interface, or functionality". MHRA warns about "Function Creep", where added functionality "leads to product claims that fall within the scope of medical device regulations". MDCG §8: "A change to or the addition of functionality to a software may lead it to be qualified as MDSW". [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA intended-purpose guidance, "Function Creep"][mhra-ip], [MDCG §8][mdcg] |

## 3. Do / don't table: copy and marketing claims

These rules apply to every surface that defines intended purpose: in-app UI, onboarding, notifications, app-store description **and category**, landing page, ads, social media, and reposted testimonials ([MDR Art. 2(12)][mdr-oj]; [MHRA p.11][mhra]; [FDA GW 2026 §III][fda-gw]).

| Claim type | Do (wellness wording) | Don't (device trigger wording) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core purpose | "Personalised training, nutrition and recovery coaching for general fitness and sport performance" | "For patients with …", "clinically proven", "medical-grade", "clinically accurate", "replaces your physio / doctor" | FDA GW 2026 disqualifiers include "claims of clinical equivalence, clinical accuracy, medical or clinical grade". MHRA indicative words: "Clinically proven…", "Clinical Trials Evidence…". [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA p.11][mhra] |
| Fitness outcomes | "Get stronger", "build endurance", "improve your padel performance", "move better", "improve general mobility" | "Treats", "cures", "heals", "fixes [condition]", "restores function after injury" | FDA GW §III examples. MHRA treatment indicative words: "Cure/cures", "Heals", "Eliminates", "Fights", "Combats", "Controls", "Counteracts", "Clears". [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MHRA p.22][mhra] |
| Pain / injury | "Train around what feels limited; see a professional about pain" | "Reduces / relieves pain", "rehab", "rehabilitation programme", "physiotherapy", "recover from your injury" | MHRA indicative "Reduce pain". MDCG MSK rehab example. [MHRA p.22][mhra], [MDCG §3.2][mdcg] |
| Prevention | Generic advice ("warming up is part of smart training") | "Prevents [disease]", "protects against", "avoids", "stops" a disease. Disease-risk prediction | MHRA prevention indicative words: "Avoids…", "Combats…", "Controls…", "Protects against…", "Stops…". Prevention of disease needs "a link to specific disease/s". [MHRA p.19][mhra] |
| Injury prevention | — | — | **Grey**: outside the EU/UK prevention limb (disease only), but no FDA text was found. See §2 |
| Assessment | "Check-in", "how did that feel?", "track your sleep" | "Diagnose", "detects", "screening", "symptom checker", "triage", "prognosis", "predicts", "risk of …", "spots", "finds", "measures [clinical parameter]" | MHRA diagnosis indicative words list. MHRA App. 1 indicative words: "Triage", "Self assessment", "Working diagnosis", "Differential diagnosis". [MHRA p.20][mhra], [MHRA App. 1][mhra-sym] |
| Monitoring | "Your training load and recovery trends" | "Monitors your [condition]", "alarms", "alerts you when your values are abnormal" | MHRA monitoring indicative words: "Check", "Alarms". [MHRA p.21][mhra], [FDA GW][fda-gw] |
| Nutrition | "Healthy eating", "hit your protein target", "manage your weight goal" | "Diabetic meal plan", "for IBS", "treat obesity", "lower your blood pressure" | [FDA GW §III][fda-gw], [FDA MMA App. A #6][fda-mma] |
| Sleep / mind | "Better sleep habits", "relax", "manage stress" | "Treat insomnia", "for anxiety / depression" | [FDA GW §III][fda-gw], [MHRA p.22][mhra] |
| Disease references in general | None (EU/UK) | Any named disease as a target ("helps living well with type 2 diabetes") | US: "may help living well with" is a wellness category FDA does not enforce, but it is still a device under §520(o)(1)(B). EU/UK have no equivalent. [FDA Cures §IV.B.2][fda-cures], [FDA GW][fda-gw] |
| Disclaimers | Use one (e.g. "not a medical device; not medical advice") **in addition to** clean claims | Relying on a disclaimer while medical claims appear elsewhere | MHRA: "General disclaimers (for example 'this product is not a medical device') are not acceptable if medical claims are made or implied elsewhere". "For information only" does not exempt a qualifying app. [MHRA p.11][mhra]. FDA GW: labeling must not "exceed the product's stated intended use". [FDA GW][fda-gw] |
| Testimonials | Fitness and performance testimonials | Reposting "fixed my back pain" or "cured my …" reviews | MHRA: "Anecdotal quotes and testimonials are considered to be implied claims by the manufacturer if they are repeated in product literature". [MHRA p.11][mhra] |
| Store category | Health & Fitness-type category | Medical category | MHRA lists "App store description and category" as promotional material that defines purpose. [MHRA p.11][mhra] |

## 4. Structural rules the sources impose (beyond individual features)

| Rule | Implication for Stamina | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Qualification follows intended purpose, not risk | Low risk does not keep a disease- or injury-directed feature out of scope, and high risk does not pull a wellness feature in. Safety features (screening, red flags) are judged by how they are framed (see §2 escalation row) [INFERENCE from the MDCG statement] | MDCG §3.1: risk of harm "is not a criterion on whether the software qualifies as a medical device" [MDCG][mdcg] |
| Modules qualify separately | Adding one disease- or injury-directed module (e.g. a "rehab" or "diabetes" mode) makes **that module** a medical device. The manufacturer must then delineate module boundaries and assess interfaces | [MDCG §7][mdcg], [MHRA p.9][mhra] |
| Feature changes require re-qualification | Each new feature or claim must be checked against the boundary. MHRA names "Function Creep" as a failure mode | [MDCG §8][mdcg], [MHRA intended-purpose guidance, "Function Creep"][mhra-ip] |
| FDA wellness is a two-factor test | (1) Wellness-only intended use **and** (2) low risk: not invasive, not implanted, no technology needing specific controls. [INFERENCE] Software-only coaching with no sensors or invasive technology appears to meet factor 2 | [FDA GW §II, §IV][fda-gw] |
| Non-device ≠ unregulated | Other law still applies (e.g. product safety, data protection). MHRA cites the General Product Safety Regulations and GDPR/PECR for non-device apps | [MHRA p.37][mhra] (other regimes not researched here) |

## 5. Jurisdiction asymmetries and open points

| Point | Fact | Status |
|---|---|---|
| US latitude vs EU strictness | US: statutory healthy-lifestyle exclusion, plus enforcement discretion for disease-adjacent coaching (FDA MMA §V.B, App. B) and the "may help reduce the risk of / living well with" category. EU: no enforcement-discretion mechanism appears in MDR or MDCG 2019-11. Disease-directed function → MDSW → Rule 11 Class IIa+ | Verified ([FDA MMA][fda-mma], [FDA GW][fda-gw], [MDCG][mdcg]) |
| Injury prevention | EU/UK definitions: prevention is listed for disease only. MHRA says this explicitly. FDA: no statement found on injury-prevention claims for training software | EU/UK verified; US **unverified** |
| Exercise selection around a reported injury | No primary source addresses "exclude exercises for a flagged body part". The nearest examples are MDCG's MSK rehab MDSW (therapeutic intent) and FDA's PT-exercise app under enforcement discretion | **Not addressed**; classification is an inference |
| PAR-Q-style gating | Not addressed in EU guidance. UK and US texts support "gate and signpost" and treat individual disease-risk outputs as device-like | EU **not found**; UK/US verified |
| Pregnancy | FDA puts motivational guidance for pregnant people under enforcement discretion (may be a device). EU/UK guidance found does not address it | US verified; EU/UK **not found** |
| UK scope | MHRA guidance v1.10g covers Great Britain under UK MDR 2002. Northern Ireland follows different rules (EU MDR/IVDR) per the same document | Verified ([MHRA p.1][mhra]) |
| EU borderline manual | Manual on borderline and classification v5 (April 2026) contains no fitness or wellness app case. Its software cases (STI-prevention app, medical calculators) confirm the "individual-patient medical purpose" test | Verified ([Borderline manual v5 §1.1.9][bl-manual]) |
