# Calisthenics: review note

Draft for founder review. Sources: [1] Compendium of Physical Activities 2024, Conditioning Exercise; [2] Bellissimo et al., "The Acute Physiological and Perceptual Responses Between Bodyweight and Treadmill Running High-Intensity Interval Exercises" (Frontiers in Physiology, 2022); [3] Cin and Cabuk, "Effects of Running vs. Calisthenics-Based High-Intensity Interval Training Using the Tabata Protocol on Endurance, Strength, and Power in Gendarmerie Cadets" (2025). All URLs were opened while drafting.

**Everything except the METs is judgement.** Open literature on calisthenics as a sport is thin, and the sources do not describe a recreational session.

Basis types: **source** = stated by a cited source; **estimate** = my own number built from source statements; **heuristic** = rule of thumb, no source.

| Field | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy mix (aerobic / repeated sprint / explosive) | 30 / 40 / 30 | **Estimate**, no source gives a split. [2]: 12 active adults, 'all-out' calisthenic intervals (60 s on / 60 s marching) reached 77% of VO2peak, 88% of peak heart rate and blood lactate 11.2 mmol/l, i.e. vigorous with a large anaerobic share. [3]: 20 s on / 10 s off calisthenics intervals, in military cadets. Sets of push-ups, pull-ups and dips with rests are repeated short efforts (judgement). |
| Key movements | push, pull, grip, overhead | **Judgement.** [1] lists push-ups and pull-ups as calisthenics examples. Grip follows from hanging and bar work; overhead from muscle-ups and handstands. No source analyses the movements of a calisthenics session. |
| High-load areas | shoulder, elbow, wrist, upper-back | **Judgement.** Upper-body pushing and pulling against bodyweight; wrist from hand-supported positions. Load only. |
| Typical activity | 60 min, moderate | **Heuristic** for a recreational session. |
| Recovery window (lower / upper) | 24 h / 48 h | **Heuristic**, not a sourced fact. Hard calisthenics loads arms and shoulders most, so the upper-body window is longer; lower-body load is modest, so 24 h. |
| Season | weather; months 4-10 | **Heuristic**: outdoor park training in Northern Hemisphere (Greece/EU) is usual April to October; indoor training continues all year. Not sourced. |
| METs easy / moderate / hard | 2.8 / 3.8 / 7.5 | **Source** [1]: 02024 calisthenics (curl ups, abdominal crunches, plank), light effort = 2.8; 02022 calisthenics (push-ups, sit ups, pull-ups, lunges), moderate effort = 3.8; 02020 calisthenics (push-ups, sit ups, pull-ups, jumping jacks, burpees, battling ropes), vigorous effort = 7.5. The three codes are the Compendium's own light / moderate / vigorous calisthenics entries, verified on the page. |

## Uncertain, for founder review

- Everything except the METs is judgement: energy mix, key movements, high-load areas, duration, recovery windows and season.
- [2] and [3] study interval protocols (12 active adults; military cadets), not recreational calisthenics sessions. The circuit-training code 02032 (6.0) was dropped in favour of the Compendium's own calisthenics entries.
- Skill-based work (handstands, levers) may change the key movements and areas; this draft is for general calisthenics.
